Understanding TF-CBT

Helping children and teens process what they have experienced.

Trauma-Focused Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (TF-CBT) is an evidence-based intervention for children and adolescents ages 6–18 and their caregivers when trauma, stress, or adversity has affected their lives.

The approach brings together trauma-sensitive techniques, cognitive behavioral strategies, family involvement, and supportive therapeutic work to address trauma-related difficulties.

Children and teens deserve support that meets them with understanding and helps them develop ways to cope with difficult experiences.

When families seek support

When might TF-CBT help?

Families may explore TF-CBT when a traumatic or stressful experience has affected a child's emotions, behavior, or relationships.

01 / Emotional well-being

Trauma-related distress

A child or teen is experiencing fear, anxiety, depression, or post-traumatic stress symptoms related to traumatic life events.

02 / Difficult experiences

Single or multiple traumas

A child or adolescent has been impacted by one traumatic experience or by several experiences over time.

03 / Family connection

Caregiver support

Caregivers want guidance with parenting and strengthening the caregiver–child relationship after a traumatic or stressful event.

What treatment involves

Practical skills and trauma-sensitive support.

TF-CBT uses a range of approaches to help children and adolescents better understand their experiences and develop skills for responding to difficult emotions.

01 / Understanding

Understanding trauma

Age-appropriate education helps children and teens make sense of trauma and its effects.

02 / Regulation

Calming & emotional regulation

Developing calming strategies and skills for recognizing and managing strong emotions.

03 / Coping

Helpful ways of thinking

Learning and practicing cognitive coping strategies to respond to thoughts and feelings more adaptively.

04 / Processing

Processing difficult experiences

Support for understanding and integrating traumatic or stressful experiences in a healthy way.

Supporting caregivers

Caregiver involvement matters, too.

TF-CBT includes participation by a non-offending caregiver. The approach can support caregivers in managing their own distress about the child's traumatic experiences while building effective parenting skills and supportive interactions.

Support extends beyond the session.

Support for a young person can also include strengthening the understanding and responsiveness of the adults around them.
