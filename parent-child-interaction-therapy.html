Parent–Child Interaction Therapy.

Parent–Child Interaction Therapy (PCIT) helps caregivers learn practical skills while supporting children who experience emotional or behavioral challenges.

Understanding PCIT

Growing connection through guided interactions.

PCIT is an evidence-based therapeutic intervention for young children, typically ages 2–7, who experience emotional and behavioral challenges. Caregivers participate directly in the treatment.

Milestone's therapists are also trained to use developmentally appropriate PCIT adaptations with children ages 8–11.

The work focuses on both the child's needs and the caregiver–child relationship.

When families seek support

Challenges PCIT may help address.

Families may explore PCIT when a child's emotional or behavioral challenges are creating stress at home or in the caregiver–child relationship.

01 / Emotions

Managing strong feelings

Difficulty regulating strong emotions or coping when things do not go as expected.

02 / Guidance

Following directions

Challenges sustaining attention, following directions, or accepting adult guidance.

03 / Anxiety

Responding to worry

Anxious behaviors, including attempts to control surroundings or interactions.

04 / Reactions

Navigating outbursts

Emotional outbursts, aggression, or physical expressions of distress.

05 / Cooperation

Reducing power struggles

Frequent power struggles between a child and caregiver.

06 / Relationships

Supporting family connection

Family relationship distress or a desire for more effective parenting strategies.

What sessions look like

Real-time coaching during play.

During PCIT coaching sessions, you and your child spend time together in a playroom. The therapist observes from a separate room through a one-way window.

Using a small “bug-in-the-ear” device, the therapist offers in-the-moment guidance as you practice skills for understanding and responding to your child's emotions and behavior.

You practice. Your therapist coaches. Together, you build skills during real interactions.

The treatment process

Two phases, with different goals.

PCIT combines relationship-building skills with consistent, supportive ways of responding to challenging behaviors.

01 / Building connection

Strengthening warmth and security

The first phase focuses on strengthening your relationship with your child. You learn and practice skills intended to help children feel calmer, more secure in their relationships, and more confident in themselves.

02 / Supporting behavior

Calm, consistent limits

The second phase focuses on responding to challenging behaviors with confidence, consistency, and appropriate discipline. Caregivers practice setting limits, guiding behavior, and helping children navigate rules and expectations.

Planning for care

Progress at your family's pace.

With consistent attendance and homework completion, PCIT can be completed in approximately 12–20 sessions. Treatment is not time-limited, and each family's needs may differ.

For more information, visit PCIT International's resource for parents (opens in a new tab).

Who PCIT supports

Typically ages 2–7: Young children and their caregivers.

Ages 8–11: Developmentally appropriate adaptations offered by Milestone therapists.
