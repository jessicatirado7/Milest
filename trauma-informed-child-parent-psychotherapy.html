Understanding child-parent psychotherapy

Support that includes both child and caregiver.

Trauma-Informed Child-Parent Psychotherapy is an evidence-based therapeutic intervention for young children from birth through age seven and their caregivers.

CPP aims to help young children understand difficult events their family has experienced, develop ways to cope with challenging emotions, and have a clearer sense of what they can expect in the future.

At the heart of CPP is attention to the child’s experiences and the strengths and relationships within the family.

When families seek support

When might CPP help?

There are many reasons a family might seek support following a difficult experience or a change in a child’s relationships and daily life.

01 / Loss & separation

Difficult life events

Loss of a loved one, separation, or other frightening or painful events.

02 / Trauma

Frightening experiences

Serious medical procedures or experiences of abuse or violence at home or in the community.

03 / Emotional well-being

Emotional and behavioral challenges

Challenging behaviors or difficulties managing emotions.

04 / Family transitions

Changes in caregiving

Changes in a child's placement or caregivers.

05 / Family health

Health-related stress

Physical or mental health difficulties affecting family members.

06 / Relationships

Caregiver–child connection

Caregivers who want support with parenting and their relationship with their child.

What treatment involves

Care for the family, together and individually.

CPP is delivered through a combination of caregiver-only sessions and caregiver–child family sessions. The approach supports a family's strengths and relationships while respecting the knowledge caregivers bring about their own children.

01 / Supporting caregivers

Caregiver-only sessions

Caregivers have space within treatment to focus on their child's experiences and their family's needs.

02 / Connecting together

Caregiver–child sessions

Family sessions bring the child and caregiver together as part of the therapeutic process.

Family-centered care

Care that builds on your family’s strengths.

CPP brings caregivers and children into the therapeutic process while making room for each family’s experiences, relationships, and needs.

For young children and caregivers.

CPP provides trauma-informed support for children from birth through age seven and their caregivers.
