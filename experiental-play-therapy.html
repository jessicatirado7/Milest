Understanding play therapy

The work and language of childhood.

Play is the work and the language of the child. It is a natural part of a child’s daily development to engage in play. Pretend play allows children to explore different identities, feelings, and experiences.

In play, children can have a brave space to lean into feelings and experiences that may be too difficult to address or explore directly.

Through play, children can feel seen, safe, and understood.

What play can make possible

Different ways to explore and express.

Imaginative play can help children explore experiences that may be difficult to describe directly.

01 / Feelings

Explore emotions

Pretend play can offer children a way to explore feelings and experiences.

02 / Experiences

Create meaningful scenarios

Through play, children may create scenarios that reflect real-life situations and themes.

03 / Connection

Invite a therapist into play

A child may invite a therapist to join these scenarios and explore what feels hard to put into words.

What sessions can look like

Making room for the child's experiences.

In experiential play therapy, children create scenarios through play and may invite a therapist into those scenarios to work through real-life situations and themes they cannot easily put into words.

Safety, acceptance, and understanding

Feeling accepted, understood, and safe during play can help children work through experiences connected to challenging emotions or behaviors.
