Social-Emotional Skill Building

Helping children explore feelings, relationships, and everyday challenges through activities, play, and the support of the people who care for them.

A thoughtful, child-centered approach

Building skills through meaningful experiences.

Our therapists weave social-emotional skill-building into individual or family therapy sessions. This may involve structured activities, pretend play, or a combination of both.

These experiences create opportunities to work on skills that can be particularly challenging for children experiencing mental health difficulties.

Care can be playful, purposeful, and rooted in connection.

What this can look like

Different ways to learn and connect.

01 / Activities

Structured experiences

Therapists may use planned activities to support social-emotional skill development during sessions.

02 / Play

Learning through pretend play

Pretend play can be another way children explore and practice skills in the therapeutic setting.

03 / Family

Caregiver participation

Whenever possible, caregivers are included to help support a child's growing understanding and use of these skills.

Additional support

Interested in a skills group?

Milestone has offered social-emotional skills groups for children in addition to support within individual and family therapy.

Availability can change. Contact the office to ask whether groups are currently being offered or whether a future group-interest list is available.

Ask about group availability

Our team can answer general questions about the service and help you learn about current opportunities.
